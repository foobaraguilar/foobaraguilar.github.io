Horses, Sailing, and Selling Wine at a Farmers Market

Finding human touch

October 16, 2026

I'd grown up working on a horse ranch. There's always something that needs doing, something that needs fixing, and usually a lot less money to spend on it than you'd like.

You get scrappy pretty quickly. You look at what you already have. You figure out what you can reuse. You try to solve the problem for as little money as possible as fast as possible because ranch animals and wildlife don't have the awareness to wait around for when you're ready.

With the small fixes, it was usually okay to fail. The cost of trying was low enough that you could give something a shot, see if it held, and try again if it didn't. But once you got something working, you wanted it to work for as long as possible. You didn't want to be back doing the same repair tomorrow. Cheap took some thought.

When actual ranch work is slow, you end up looking for odd jobs. Little things that need doing. You get used to noticing a problem and wondering whether you could take care of it yourself. I think that habit sticks with you long after you leave the ranch.

Needless to say, a lot of that feeds into my philosophy around execution. Try something, learn from it, make it hold. But I'll save that tangent for another post.

When I was traveling, I purchased a seat on a sailboat from Panama to Colombia to get around the Darién Gap. That experience stayed with me. When I came back to San Francisco, I realized I wanted a similar playing field to ranching: somewhere I could work with my hands, learn by doing, and feel connected to what was happening around me.

So I picked sailing and learned how to sail.

There's something satisfying about having to pay attention to the thing right in front of you. The wind changes. You adjust. You feel the boat respond. You're learning something you can actually feel yourself getting better at, even while there's still plenty you don't know.

Now I'm selling wine at a farmers market.

Another odd job, I guess. And another place where I get that feeling. Someone stops at the table, you talk, you figure out what they like. A bottle of wine gives you somewhere to start, but you still have to listen to the person in front of you. I like that part.

I don't think I understood the connection when I started doing each of these things. I just kept finding my way back to work that asked me to be there, pay attention, and do something useful.

The ranch, sailing, selling wine. They all make me feel connected. To the work itself, to the people around me, and to a part of myself I want to keep making room for.
