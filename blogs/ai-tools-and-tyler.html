AI/Tools

My Arsenal

Software, hardware, and context layers

October 16, 2026

I think about my setup in three layers: the software I use to do the work, the hardware I touch all day, and the context that tells me and my agents what matters.

SOFTWARE

The tools I use to think, build, communicate, and run agents.

  • Dia, Ghostty, and Herdr: My prized children. They’re central to how I browse, work in the terminal, and manage my agents.
  • Codex: For building automations and pushing PRs.
  • ChatGPT: My go-to for polishing and cleaning up my writing.
  • Claude: I like having a different model review my work for another perspective.
  • Wispr Flow: I mainly use it for long utterances, and over time I think it cuts the time I spend typing. But carefully structured, typed prompts tend to produce more accurate results, so I’m constantly balancing speaking freely against writing precisely.
  • Granola: Captures my meetings and keeps the discussion useful afterward.
  • Google Gemini: I use its meeting notes as a baseline for grading Tyler’s.
  • OpenClaw: For agent workflows, including the gateway behind Tyler.

HARDWARE

The physical setup I spend my day with.

  • MacBook Pro 14-inch (M5): Light, easy to carry, and it fits how I move around.
  • AirPods and Sony XM5s: AirPods by default. In the evenings, when my ears need a break, I switch to the Sony headphones.
  • Apple keyboard: I love how simple it is.
  • MX Master 3S: The antithesis of the Apple mouse.
  • MacBook Pro trackpad and MX Master 3S: I love switching between the two.

CONTEXT

The information around the tools matters just as much: conversations, product behavior, business definitions, project history, and the things I’ve decided are worth remembering.

  • PostHog: Product context. Its events, funnels, and retention data show what people do, where they drop off, and what brings them back, which grounds my work in actual behavior.
  • Kaelio: Business and data context. I like tools that help agents understand what the data means: the definitions, relationships, and logic behind a question.
  • Slack: At a startup, this is a big part of my working context: discussions and decisions.
  • Linear: Issues and what needs attention.
  • Google Drive: Where I return for documents and shared knowledge. I’ve tried to get on board with Notion, but Drive is what sticks.
  • A little black notebook: I handwrite my critical notes in it, and I love it. The notebook is part of my setup; what I write in it is part of my context.

TYLER

“Why don’t you just use Granola like a regular person?” — Hans, my roommate, circa 2026

Fair question. I do use Granola, and I like it. But I wanted something that also catches what I say out loud in my room, not just what happens on calls. That experiment is Tyler, my personal assistant.

Tyler is where the three layers meet. The Brio and a Raspberry Pi 3 capture what’s said in my room and on Google Meet calls, the OpenClaw gateway on the Pi 4 stores it, and I query that gateway whenever I want to get back to what was discussed. Gemini’s notes act as a baseline, so I have a reference point for reviewing what Tyler captured and where his notes could improve. The gateway also has access to my personal Google Calendar, Gmail, and Google Drive. When I’m away from my room, I talk to it over a Telegram channel and can access my room notes there.

Tyler also has a monitor, which I got for free from Facebook Marketplace. On it is a figurehead I designed, with subtle responsiveness that signals Tyler is reacting when I engage with him.

I think of my room as a context layer. I want to speak ideas aloud without worrying about losing them, and return to the conversation when it becomes useful again.

What works today: capturing, comparing, and retrieving notes.

Recently, a friend came over and Tyler transcribed most of our conversation, so I could review a summary of the key notes afterward.

What I want to build:

  • Visual context through the Brio
  • Habit tracking, like how much water I drink or how many times I pick up my phone at my desk
  • Full-duplex or half-duplex conversation, including listening and responding at the same time
