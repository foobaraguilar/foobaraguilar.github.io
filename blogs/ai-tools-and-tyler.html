My Arsenal

Software, hardware, and context layers

October 16, 2026

I think about my setup in three layers: the software I use to do the work, the hardware I interact with, and the context that helps me understand what matters.

Software

The tools I use to think, build, communicate, and run agents.

  • Dia + Ghostty + Herdr — My prized children. They’re central to how I browse, work in the terminal, and manage my agents.
  • ChatGPT — My go-to for polishing and cleaning up my writing.
  • Codex — For building automations and pushing PRs.
  • Claude — I like using different models to review my work and get another perspective.
  • Wispr Flow — I mainly use it for long utterances, and over time I think it reduces the time I spend typing. I also think typing out clear, carefully structured prompts can lead to more accurate results. I’m constantly working on that balance between speaking freely and writing precisely.
  • Granola — For capturing meetings and keeping the discussion useful afterward.
  • Google Gemini — Its meeting notes give Tyler a relative benchmark for comparing his own notes.
  • OpenClaw — For agent workflows, including the gateway behind Tyler.

Hardware

The physical setup I spend my day with.

  • MacBook Pro 14-inch (M5) — I love this thing. It’s light, easy to carry, and works well for how I move around.
  • AirPods + Sony XM5 headphones — I love my AirPods. In the evenings, when the insides of my ears need a break, I switch to my Sony headphones.
  • Apple keyboard + trackpad — Ride or die for the simple Apple keyboard. I like moving between the keyboard and trackpad.
  • MX Master 3S — My other ride or die.
  • Raspberry Pi 4 + Raspberry Pi 3 — The Pi 4 runs Tyler’s OpenClaw gateway. The Pi 3 runs the Brio webcam note-taker.
  • Logitech Brio webcam and microphone — The input for Tyler’s note-taking setup. Having a camera also gives me room to explore visual context later.

Context layers

The information around the tools matters to me: conversations, product behavior, business definitions, project history, and the things I’ve decided are worth remembering.

  • PostHog — Product context. I use it to understand what people do, where they drop off, and what brings them back. Its events, funnels, and retention data help ground that work in actual behavior.
  • Kaelio — Business and data context. I like tools that help agents understand what the data means, including the definitions, relationships, and logic behind a question.
  • Slack + Linear — When I’m at a startup, these are a big part of my working context: discussions, decisions, issues, and what needs attention.
  • Google Drive — Where I tend to return for documents and shared knowledge. I’ve tried to get on board with Notion, but Drive is what sticks.
  • Meeting and conversation notes — Granola notes and the conversations I capture through Tyler help me return to what was discussed.
  • A little black notebook — I handwrite my critical notes in it. I love that notebook. The physical notebook is part of my setup; what I write in it is part of my context.

Tyler

“Why don’t you just use Granola like a regular person?” — Hans, my roommate, circa 2026.

Tyler is my personal assistant and an experiment in bringing these layers together.

Right now, he makes meeting notes and compares them with Google Gemini’s notes as a relative benchmark. That comparison gives me a reference point for reviewing what he captures and where his notes could improve.

I query his OpenClaw gateway to retrieve stored conversations from my room or Google Meet calls. That’s what I use him for today: getting back to what was discussed.

I think of my room as a context layer. I want to be able to speak ideas aloud without worrying about losing them, and return to the conversation when it becomes useful again.

The Brio gives me the possibility of exploring visual context. Over time, I’d also like Tyler to help track habits, support conversations with multiple people, and listen and respond at the same time. Those are things I want to build toward; the current focus is capturing, comparing, and retrieving notes.
