My Arsenal

hardware, software, and a little black notebook

October 16, 2026

“Why don't you just use Granola like a regular person?” — Hans, my roommate, circa 2026.

Hardware

  • MacBook Pro 14-inch (M5) — I love this thing. It's perfect for me: light, and easy to carry everywhere.
  • AirPods — I love my AirPods.
  • Sony XM5 headphones — Love these too.
  • Apple keyboard + mouse — Ride or die for the simple Apple keyboard. I love jumping between the keyboard and mouse.
  • MX Master 3S — My other ride or die.

Tools

  • Dia + Ghostty + Herdr — My prized children.
  • Codex — For working on this website and turning rough notes into blog drafts.
  • Claude — I've used it for race preparation, including training and fueling plans.
  • Wispr Flow — For voice dictation.
  • OpenClaw — The gateway behind Tyler. More on him below.
  • Arcade MCP connector — Another tool I love having in the setup.
  • A little black notebook — I handwrite my critical notes in it. I love that notebook.

At a startup

When I'm at a startup, I like Slack and Linear. I've tried to get on board with Notion, but it never sticks. I end up defaulting to Google Drive. Some sort of self-driving agent is still a must.

Tyler

Then there's Tyler, my personal assistant. Right now, he just takes notes. Tyler runs on a Raspberry Pi as an OpenClaw gateway. A second Pi, a Raspberry Pi 3, runs the Brio webcam note-taker.

I query the OpenClaw gateway to access stored conversation notes from my room or Google Meet calls. That's what I use Tyler for today: getting back to what was discussed.

The reason I use a Brio is that, theoretically, I can use it for multimodal capture beyond notes. Having a camera gives me the possibility of working with visual context as well. That's part of what I want to explore with this setup.

Over time, I want him to track my habits. With open-source annotation tools, that feels easier to build and support. It's something I want to add as I develop Tyler further.

I also want to add full-duplex conversation, so Tyler can listen and respond at the same time, along with support for conversations involving multiple people. Those are still plans. Right now, I'm constrained by the Raspberry Pi 3 running the webcam note-taker.
