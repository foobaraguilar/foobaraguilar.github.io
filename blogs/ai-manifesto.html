A Manifesto: My 33 Million Problems With the AI Revolution

My beef with AI

June 5, 2026

I took time off and traveled — Central America, South America, Patagonia. Somewhere in there I picked up sailing and started running ultramarathons. It was brutal. But it built something: a bias toward learning by doing.

I started in enterprise. Microsoft. Accenture. I learned how data moves at scale, how decisions get made when resources aren't a constraint. Then I moved to the frontier — a YC-backed robotics startup, then a geometric deep learning company. I was inside the data layer: egocentric video pipelines, annotation schemas, QA frameworks for manipulation tasks. Close to the edge of what AI can currently do.

But the longer I stayed at the frontier, the more I kept thinking about everyone who would never benefit from it.

The AI being built is extraordinarily powerful. It's also being built almost exclusively for organizations that already have the infrastructure to absorb it. That assumption eliminates most of the economy before the conversation even starts.

There are 33 million small businesses in the United States. They employ 46% of the private sector workforce and account for nearly 44% of economic activity. They're running on spreadsheets, email chains, and institutional knowledge locked inside one person's head — not because they're behind, but because they're thriving without any of it. Decades of expertise. Real customer relationships. Deep domain knowledge.

The economics of compute don't scale down. The gap between enterprise and everyone else isn't closing. It's accelerating. And the pitch coming out of Silicon Valley is always the same: migrate to our platform, connect your APIs, retrain your staff. For a business with six employees and twenty years of process baked into muscle memory, that's not a solution.

I thought I understood the resistance. I had frameworks. Then I got closer and realized: it's not about tooling or cost. It's about trust — and there's still more I don't understand yet.

So I stopped theorizing and got inside the problem.

I recently teamed up with one of San Francisco's most established rare coin and bullion dealers to lift their operations — and I'm building automation infrastructure for my sailing school.

These aren't failing businesses. They're thriving ones, completely untouched by the last decade of operational technology. I'm not there as a consultant with a deck. I'm doing the work — mapping every manual process from the inside, automating what can be automated, building a real playbook for how AI actually reaches the shop floor.
